# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the project management process materials used by OctoAcme. The documents provide a practical framework for turning ideas into clearly scoped work, coordinating cross-functional teams, and delivering outcomes with predictable communication and quality checks.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme’s workflow begins with project initiation, where the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, defines success metrics, and creates a lightweight one-pager with milestones, risks, and resource needs. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning, where the backlog is prioritized, work is estimated, acceptance criteria are defined, dependencies are identified, and a release plan is mapped out. This creates a shared understanding of what needs to be built, when it should be delivered, and who owns what.

During execution and tracking, the project team runs regular standups, manages blockers, and uses a project board to keep work visible across backlog, ready, in-progress, review, QA, and done states. The project management model emphasizes clear ownership and coordination across roles, from Product and Project leadership through engineering and QA, so that technical work remains aligned to business outcomes. The communication cadence includes daily or team-level standups, weekly delivery syncs, stakeholder updates, and escalation paths that raise issues to the right decision-makers as needed.

Risk management and communication are treated as part of delivery rather than separate activities. OctoAcme maintains a risk register that tracks impact, likelihood, ownership, mitigation, and status, and it escalates issues through a clear path from team-level triage to project leadership and, when necessary, sponsor-level intervention. Communication is standardized through weekly status updates, milestone-based updates, and incident communication templates that keep stakeholders informed, reduce surprises, and support rapid decision-making.

Release and deployment follow a consistent checklist to confirm readiness, validate the change in staging, and carry out production deployment with smoke tests, post-deploy verification, and stakeholder notifications. Retrospectives and continuous improvement close the loop by capturing what went well, what could be improved, and what action items should be tracked in the backlog. This creates a repeatable delivery model that balances speed, accountability, quality, and stakeholder transparency across the full project lifecycle.
